# Adaptive Electroformed 3D Power Topology Lite (AE3PT-Lite): Business Funder Brief

## What a Small Investment Buys, What Evidence It Produces, and What It Does Not Prove

> **Plain-language proposal:** Fund a 24-week student demonstration that tests whether a copper-coated 3D-printed electrical path can use material efficiently and be repaired after controlled damage.

**AE3PT-Lite** means **Adaptive Electroformed 3D Power Topology Lite**, the reduced classroom version of the research idea.

The proposed classroom project operates at five volts and no more than two amperes. It is not a high-power prototype. Its purpose is to reduce uncertainty cheaply before anyone considers a larger technical programme.

---

## 1. The Opportunity

Electrical conductors are usually manufactured as solid metal parts or conventional wiring. A printed plastic form with copper added only where needed may offer:

- lower use of virgin copper;
- shapes that are difficult to machine;
- integrated mounting or identification features;
- visible repair zones;
- local restoration instead of full replacement;
- useful teaching and workforce-development value.

These possibilities are **hypotheses**, meaning testable ideas, not proven commercial benefits.

---

## 2. The Demonstration

Students manufacture and compare:

1. a straight reference path;
2. a material-saving path;
3. a repair-ready path.

Three copies of each design are made. One repair-ready sample is measured, damaged, repaired, and measured again.

The experiment records:

- electrical resistance;
- sample temperature;
- plastic and copper mass;
- manufacturing time;
- failed samples;
- repair time and material;
- **direct cost**, meaning cost clearly assigned to this project;
- difference between prediction and measurement.

---

## 3. Funding Request

**USD** means **United States dollars**. **Contingency** means budget reserved for failures and uncertain cost.

### One student team

Approximately USD $1,555 when a computer, printer, ordinary tools, and supervised plating facilities are already available.

### Four teams or about 12 students

Approximately USD $7,700, including shared equipment, supervised plating access, teaching consumables, and contingency.

The [Student Bill of Materials](bill-of-materials.md) provides details and purchase gates.

---

## 4. What the Funder Receives

- nine traceable functional samples;
- original, damaged, and repaired demonstration states;
- open calculation and analysis software;
- calibration evidence, meaning records showing how instruments were compared with references;
- manufacturing and failure records;
- direct cost report;
- repair-versus-replacement comparison;
- student report and presentation;
- recommended next decision: stop, repeat, or expand.

The project also develops student capability across manufacturing, electronics, software, measurement, finance, and communication.

---

## 5. Decision Gates

Funding can be released in stages.

Funding-release labels use **F** for **Funding** followed by the sequence number. Project decision gates use **G** for **Gate** followed by the sequence number. These are internal project labels, not accounting or engineering standards.

| Release | Amount guide | Evidence required |
|---|---:|---|
| F0 — Planning | $150 | approved scope, risk plan, designs to be compared |
| F1 — Tools | $350 | logger and fixture design, reference calculations |
| F2 — Printing | $250 | dimensional coupons and frozen designs |
| F3 — Plating | $400 | approved laboratory process and successful seed coupon |
| F4 — Testing and repair | $250 | traceable samples and working data system |
| F5 — Reporting reserve | $155 | completed dataset and review schedule |
| **Team total** | **$1,555** | all stages |

Staged release prevents optional equipment from consuming the budget before core evidence exists.

---

## 6. Business Measures

### Cost per successful sample

Consumed cost divided by the number of samples meeting the classroom criteria.

### Repair cost

Inspection, cleaning, mask, plating, testing, and labour time.

### Replacement cost

New print, seed, plating, inspection, testing, and delay.

### Material efficiency

Useful electrical performance compared with copper mass.

### Technical yield

The proportion of manufactured samples that pass.

### Learning yield

The proportion of students who can reproduce and explain the result. This matters because the programme is partly an education investment.

---

## 7. Important Economic Terms

**Capital equipment** is reusable, such as a printer. **Consumables** are used by the project, such as filament and masks.

**Contingency** is reserved budget for uncertainty and failures.

**Payback** describes how many repairs or uses are needed to recover extra initial cost.

**Return on Investment**, abbreviated ROI, compares benefit with investment. A classroom experiment does not provide enough evidence for a reliable commercial ROI forecast.

**Sunk cost** is money already spent and not recoverable. It should not force continued funding when evidence says the idea should stop.

---

## 8. Risks

| Risk | Business effect | Control |
|---|---|---|
| coating is inconsistent | low manufacturing yield | coupon-first process and three replicates |
| measurement is unreliable | decisions cannot be trusted | calibration and four-wire measurement |
| repair does not restore temperature | repair claim fails | electrical and thermal repair criteria |
| process cost is higher than expected | weak economic case | actual cost log and staged funding |
| laboratory access is delayed | schedule delay | confirm access at Gate G0 |
| students add unnecessary complexity | budget and schedule overrun | fixed 5 V/2 A scope |
| results are overstated | reputational risk | lecturer and non-technical review |

---

## 9. What Success Looks Like

Success does not require every technical hypothesis to be correct.

The funded project succeeds when:

- the demonstration remains safe and within scope;
- all samples are traceable;
- measurements are repeatable enough to compare designs;
- repair is evaluated honestly;
- costs include failed work;
- the next decision is supported by evidence;
- students can explain the result to a business audience.

---

## 10. Stop, Repeat, or Expand

### Stop

Choose stop when the coating is unreliable, measurement cannot separate designs, repair repeatedly fails, or cost is clearly unattractive.

### Repeat

Choose repeat when the idea remains plausible but variation, fixture quality, or process control prevents a clear conclusion.

### Expand

Choose expand only when:

- multiple samples pass;
- the repair result is repeatable;
- cost categories are understood;
- the simple model is useful;
- safety and process partners are available;
- the next experiment has a specific unanswered question.

Expansion should increase one dimension at a time: more samples, another material, more repair cycles, or a moderately higher current under new approval. It should not jump directly to vehicles or industrial power.

---

## 11. Questions Funders Should Ask

1. What result would cause the team to stop?
2. How many samples passed and failed?
3. Were failed costs included?
4. Was repair cheaper only because labour was excluded?
5. Did the repair restore both electrical and thermal performance?
6. Which equipment can be reused next year?
7. Which result depends most on an assumption?
8. Can a person outside engineering understand the evidence?
9. What is the smallest next experiment?
10. Who owns safety, data, and process responsibility?

---

## 12. Recommended Funding Decision

Fund the classroom demonstration when suitable supervision and plating access already exist. Use staged release and require evidence at each gate. Treat the result as a low-cost option to learn, train students, and decide whether a larger experiment is justified—not as proof of commercial readiness.
